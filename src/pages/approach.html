How the methods work, in plain language

Reem's focus is IFS-informed EMDR: EMDR run with parts awareness, so the protective parts of you get consulted rather than overridden. It sits alongside two other approaches. Somatic Experiencing works with the nervous system directly, in small doses, to widen what you can tolerate. NARM works with the patterns that formed in childhood and how they still shape your present. Brainspotting comes in where it helps.

NARM, the NeuroAffective Relational Model

NARM starts from curiosity rather than diagnosis. Where did I learn this pattern? How was it serving me? Is it still?

The invitation is to notice how a pattern keeps you from being present in your own life, and to discover through felt sense which path leads toward thriving. It is less about excavating the past than about meeting what the past installed, now, with attention.

Reem is a NARM therapist and NARM is the spine of most of her work with developmental trauma.

Best when

  • The trouble is a lifelong pattern, not a single event.
  • You understand your history intellectually and nothing has changed.
  • Self-criticism is doing most of the talking.

Somatic Experiencing

Somatic Experiencing, developed by Dr Peter Levine, treats trauma as something the body is still holding rather than a story the mind needs to retell. Sessions track physical sensation and the small movements a threat response never got to finish.

The work is titrated, meaning slow and in doses, because a nervous system that gets flooded learns that attention to the body is dangerous. Done at the right pace, it widens your window of tolerance and your resilience.

Reem is a Somatic Experiencing Practitioner (SEP).

Best when

  • Shock trauma is in the picture: an accident, an assault, a medical event.
  • You go numb, or flood, when you talk about hard things.
  • Anxiety lives in your chest and stomach more than your thoughts.

EMDR

EMDR helps the brain finish processing experiences it stored in raw form. Reem is EMDR certified and an EMDRIA Approved Consultant, trained in ASSYST, PRECI and IGTP-OTS protocols and in IFS-informed EMDR.

Her emphasis is stabilisation first: acute stress, ongoing stress, feeling overwhelmed, loss. Only with that footing does the processing work become useful rather than destabilising.

Therapists seeking consultation on their own EMDR practice can work with Reem as a consultant.

Best when

  • Specific memories intrude, or certain triggers reliably hijack you.
  • You are in an ongoing stressful situation and need footing before insight.
  • Talking about it has stopped helping.

IFS-informed EMDR, where most of the work happens

Standard EMDR asks you to hold a memory and let the processing run. For people with developmental trauma that instruction often meets an internal objection: a part of you that has spent years keeping this material at a distance and has good reasons.

Internal Family Systems gives us language for that. Rather than pushing past the protector, we get to know it. What is it afraid will happen if you go near this? What does it need in place first? Processing starts when the protective parts agree to it, not when the protocol says it is time.

Reem's emphasis is phases one and two, the history-taking and preparation phases, because that is where most stalled EMDR actually stalls. Build enough stability and enough internal agreement there, and the later phases stop being a fight.

She completed steps one through three of IFS-informed EMDR training in 2025, is EMDR certified and an EMDRIA Approved Consultant, and consults with other therapists on exactly this.

What this looks like in session

  • Naming the parts in the room: the one that wants to heal, the one that wants to leave, the one that thinks this is self-indulgent.
  • Unblending, so you can look at a part rather than speak as it.
  • Asking permission from protectors before any target gets processed.
  • Resourcing and stabilisation that a protective part actually trusts.
  • Slowing or pausing processing when a part objects, and treating that objection as information rather than resistance.

Best when

  • EMDR has been tried before and went nowhere, or went badly.
  • You flood, dissociate or go blank when you approach the material.
  • Part of you wants this and part of you really does not.
  • The trauma is developmental and layered rather than a single event.

How the three fit together

They are not three separate treatments you pick between. In practice they interlock: NARM asks what pattern is running and where it was learned, Somatic Experiencing sets the pace your nervous system can hold, and IFS-informed EMDR does the processing once the first two have made room for it.

Which one leads on a given day depends on what you arrive with. A week of acute stress calls for stabilisation. A recurring relational pattern calls for curiosity. A memory that keeps intruding calls for processing, once the parts holding it are on board.

Questions about the methods

What is IFS-informed EMDR?

EMDR practised with Internal Family Systems awareness. Before any memory is processed, the protective parts of you that object to the work are identified and consulted, and resourcing is built that those parts trust. It makes EMDR usable for people with developmental trauma, where standard protocol timing often destabilises rather than resolves. Reem Glasco focuses on phases one and two, the preparation phases.

Why not just do standard EMDR?

For a single discrete trauma with a stable nervous system, standard protocol often works well. When trauma is developmental and layered, parts of you have organised around keeping it away, and processing that skips those parts tends to flood, stall or stop working. Consulting them first is slower at the start and faster overall.

Do NARM, Somatic Experiencing and EMDR get used in the same session?

Often, yes. Reem is a NARM therapist, a Somatic Experiencing Practitioner and EMDR certified, so the approaches move with what you bring. NARM tracks the pattern, Somatic Experiencing sets the pacing, IFS-informed EMDR handles processing when there is room for it.
